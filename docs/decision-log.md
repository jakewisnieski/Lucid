# Decision log

The *why* behind Lucid's load-bearing decisions. Product decisions are charted in detail on the [wayfinder map](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues?q=label%3Awayfinder%3Amap); this log records the ones worth reading in one place. Newest last.

---

### 1. Adopt the full SDLC / GitHub workflow from day one
Every change reaches `main` only through a passing, reviewed PR; branch protection was turned on while `main` was a clean scaffold. **Why:** the process is easiest to make real on an empty repo, and the audit trail (issue → PR → review → merge) is itself a deliverable. See [`github-workflow.md`](github-workflow.md).

### 2. Plan on a wayfinder map (GitHub Issues), not straight into code
Product decisions are charted as a `wayfinder:map` issue with child decision tickets, worked one at a time and grounded in primary sources. **Why:** the idea is large and foggy; deciding before building keeps the first slice honest and the reasoning inspectable.

### 3. Slice 1 is single-user / local; security & multi-user are Slice 2 — but we design to scale into them
The first slice is used personally and handed to no one, so auth, access control, and multi-tenant security are deferred. **Why:** it keeps Slice 1 small and shippable. **Constraint:** the architecture must not preclude the Slice 2 jump to security + multi-user — design the seams for it now, don't wall them off.

### 4. Scoring: an independent critic plus hard gates — not a single averaged number
A backlog item is a **user story + acceptance criteria**, scored **INVEST-primary** with the 29148 §5.2.5 characteristics as the deeper checks on the text and ACs. Deterministic linters run first (cheap, explainable) and either hard-fail or hand flags as evidence to an **independent critic pass** that renders per-characteristic **pass/weak/fail** verdicts with rationale and a fix. "Sprint-ready" is decided by **hard gates** (no placeholders, Singular, Verifiable, Valuable, no critical ambiguity); a weighted readiness score only ranks what to fix next. The same generate→lint→critic split runs once more at the **set level**, after items clear their gates. **Why:** a single 0–100 average lets an ambiguous requirement hide behind otherwise-good scores, and a generator grading its own output inflates. Gates keep the bar honest, an independent critic makes the "generator/critic split" mean something, and per-characteristic verdicts backed by evidence make every score correctable — which is Lucid's whole premise. See [#7](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/7), grounded in [#6](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/6).

### 5. Persist the `owner_id` seam everywhere, and version item revisions, from day one
Slice-1 data lives in Postgres via Prisma: a seeded local **User** owns everything, with **`owner_id` on every table (denormalized onto child rows)**, and items are **versioned** — content and acceptance criteria on an immutable `ItemRevision`, structure (type/parent) on the `Item` identity, with each `Assessment` bound to the exact revision it scored. **Why:** the `owner_id`-everywhere shape turns the Slice-2 multi-user jump into a config change — enable Postgres row-level security keyed on `owner_id` — instead of a schema migration and backfill, honoring the "design the seam, don't wall it off" constraint. And versioning items is what lets Lucid *show its work* and *learn from corrections* — clean before/after re-score diffs — rather than burying the analyst's edits. See [#9](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/9), building on [#5](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/5) and [#8](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/8).

### 6. Claude via the Vercel AI SDK; tiered models; ZDR keys the security seam
Slice 1's generator and critic run on **Anthropic Claude** behind the **Vercel AI SDK** (provider swap is config), with a **tiered** model split — **Sonnet 4.6** drafts, **Opus 4.8** judges (adaptive thinking) — plus the confidential-input posture that seeds Slice-2 security: **keys server-side only, zero-data-retention-capable models only** (Fable 5 excluded on that ground). **Why:** the critic is the load-bearing judgment pass, so the quality budget belongs there while generation stays cheap; the AI SDK gives Zod-validated structured output and Next.js streaming (the *show its work* principle) without hard-coupling to one provider; and choosing ZDR-eligible models plus server-side keys now means the multi-tenant jump is a policy change, not a provider migration. The one non-portable seam — Claude-specific prompt caching and thinking config — is isolated behind `providerOptions.anthropic`. See [#10](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/10), grounded in the Claude API reference and building on [#7](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/7) and [#8](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/8).

### 7. Slice-1 pipeline: five explicit stages, streamed and persisted; corrections re-enter locally
Pasted input runs through five persisted stages — **understand** (restate + real goal + surfaced contradictions) → **decompose** (Epic/Feature/Story/AC) → per-item **lint → critic → gate+score** (parallel fan-out) → once-per-backlog **set-lint → set-critic → readiness** → a dedicated **clarify** pass emitting a grounded clarifying-questions list (each question linked to its source flag or `gap`-tagged). Stages stream and persist as they land; generator and critic passes stay independent; contradictions are surfaced, not reconciled. A correction re-enters at item-lint for the one edited `ItemRevision` and marks the backlog `set-score stale` — the costly set-level critic (and the clarify pass that depends on it) re-run only on an explicit "re-score backlog" action. **Why:** the front-of-house *understand* stage and the *dedicated* (rather than flag-derived) clarify stage are where a BA assistant earns its keep — naming the real goal and the *gaps nobody wrote down* — so they justify the extra passes; making every stage a persisted, streamed step is what lets Lucid *show its work* and lets one corrected item re-score without re-running the whole backlog; and gating the expensive set-critic behind an explicit action keeps the Opus budget under the analyst's control. The named trade is five LLM stages (three generator, two critic) for that transparency — bounded by cheap-tier generation, a once-per-backlog set pass, and rubric-prefix caching. See [#11](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/11), building on [#7](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/7), [#9](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/9) and [#10](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/10).

### 8. Ingest one pasted input; export a clean backlog and a separate quality report, both projecting one score
Slice 1 takes **a single plaintext paste per run** into one `SourceInput` — raw text straight into the `understand` stage, with **no bespoke Slack/email parser** (that stage already absorbs headers, quoted replies, signatures, and the contradictions *within* one input), and cross-source contradiction synthesis left deferred. It exports **two markdown files**: a handoff-clean **`backlog.md`** — the Epic→Feature→Story→AC tree, each item carrying a stable ID and a compact readiness/gate badge — and a **`report.md`** — the real-goal summary, surfaced contradictions, and per-item verdicts, flags, readiness and rationale, keyed by the same IDs — alongside that same scoring surfaced **live in-app**. **Why:** paste-in / markdown-out is the whole Slice-1 I/O surface (live connectors and CSV/Jira export are Slice 2+), and the `understand` stage makes a dedicated parser redundant. Splitting the clean backlog from the report keeps the deliverable pasteable while preserving the full critic detail for anyone auditing *how* a score was derived — and grounding all three surfaces (app, backlog badge, report) in the **single `Assessment` per scored `ItemRevision`** from #5/#9 means they render the *same values at three depths* and can never disagree. Because an export is a snapshot over streamed, persisted stages, each file carries a **provenance header — export timestamp, the backlog state it reflects, and whether the set-score was stale** (#11) — so an analyst walking the paper trail always sees when an export was taken and what it captured, and a stale report never masquerades as authoritative. See [#13](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/13), building on [#9](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/9) and [#11](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/11).

### 9. Correction: the analyst owns the gate, the critic keeps its verdict — every override is on the record
Slice 1's human-in-the-loop correction gives the analyst two loops — on the **generated item** (accept, edit into a new `ItemRevision`, or soft-reject with history retained) and on the **critic's findings** (fix, or **disagree / won't-fix with a rationale**). Correctable scope is **content plus light structure** — edit text/ACs, reparent, change type — with split/merge/add deferred. When the analyst dissents from a flag, the override flips that item's **gate to analyst-accepted**, but the critic's original verdict and readiness score **stay recorded and visible** (`critic: weak · analyst-accepted`); nothing is silently rewritten. After an edit the deterministic **lint re-runs live** and the item's critic verdict goes stale, re-run by an on-demand **per-item re-score**, while the expensive set-critic and clarify stay gated behind an explicit backlog re-score (#11). Every correction is persisted as a `Correction` bound to the new revision, carrying a rationale — required on a reject or override, optional on an edit. **Why:** "letting the analyst correct it" is Lucid's whole premise, but a correction loop that lets the human silently wave away every flag makes the quality score theater, while one that lets the human change nothing is paternalistic to a sharp BA. Recorded dissent resolves the tension — the human has final say over the gate (the analyst's judgment is authoritative, per the role split), while the critic's independent verdict survives in the audit trail, so a reader can always see where the machine and the human disagreed and why. Making the per-item critic re-run on demand rather than automatically extends #11's discipline of keeping the Opus budget under the analyst's finger, and capturing a rationale on every override feeds the paper trail and preserves the deferred "learn from corrections" seam without building it yet. See [#12](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/12), building on [#7](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/7), [#9](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/9) and [#11](https://github.com/jakewisnieski/Lucid/issues/11).
